You are reading one chapter of a dense non-fiction book. Analyze it carefully and produce the following, in this order. 
THESIS — State the chapter's central argument in one sentence.
OUTLINE — Provide a deductive outline of the chapter's reasoning in complete sentences. Show how premises lead to conclusions. Aim for 300-500 words depending on the chapter's density. Do not merely list topics covered — reconstruct the logical structure of the argument.
KEY CONCEPTS — List any important terms, frameworks, or distinctions the chapter introduces or relies on, with brief working definitions (2-3 sentences each). Skip this section if the chapter introduces no notable concepts.
KEY PASSAGES — Reproduce the 4-5 most important paragraphs from the chapter in full, unabridged. Select for argumentative weight — the passages where the author's core claims are most clearly and forcefully stated. Briefly note (one sentence) why each passage matters.
CRITICAL QUESTIONS — Identify 1-2 places where the argument is most vulnerable: where evidence is thin, where a key assumption is doing heavy lifting, or where reasonable people might push back. Frame these as questions, not dismissals.
